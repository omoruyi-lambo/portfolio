How Much Does an E-commerce Website Cost in Nigeria in 2026?

Omoruyi Isaiah — Lambo Code Benin City, Nigeria
Published August 2026

Selling online in Nigeria has moved from optional to essential. Whether you run a retail store, a fashion brand, or a product-based business, an e-commerce website lets you reach customers across Nigeria and beyond. But what does it actually cost to build one?

What Makes E-commerce Different from Regular Websites?

An e-commerce website isn't just a brochure — it's a sales engine. It needs to handle products, inventory, customer accounts, shopping carts, payments, orders, and sometimes shipping. This complexity is why e-commerce development costs more than a standard business website.

The key components that add cost include:

  • Product management system — Adding, editing, and organizing products with images, descriptions, prices, and variants.
  • Shopping cart functionality — Letting customers add items, adjust quantities, and proceed to checkout.
  • Payment integration — Connecting to Nigerian payment gateways like Paystack or Flutterwave.
  • Order management — Tracking orders, sending confirmations, and managing fulfillment.
  • Customer accounts — Letting users save addresses, view order history, and manage preferences.

E-commerce Website Pricing in Nigeria

Basic Online Store (Small Catalog)

₦400,000 – ₦600,000

Suitable for businesses with 20-50 products. Includes product listings, shopping cart, Paystack payment integration, order management, and basic customer accounts. This is ideal for small retail businesses, fashion brands, or product sellers starting their online presence. Built with a focus on mobile experience since most Nigerian shoppers use phones.

Standard E-commerce Platform (Medium Catalog)

₦700,000 – ₦1,200,000

For businesses with 50-200 products. Includes advanced features like product categories, filters, search functionality, customer reviews, wishlist, discount codes, and inventory tracking. The admin dashboard is more robust, making it easier to manage a larger catalog and track sales performance.

Custom E-commerce Solution (Large Scale)

₦1,500,000 – ₦3,000,000+

Full custom e-commerce platform built for businesses with complex needs. Includes multi-vendor support, advanced shipping calculations, integration with accounting or ERP systems, custom payment flows, API integrations, and scalable architecture designed to grow with your business. This is for serious e-commerce operations.

Payment Integration in Nigeria

Payment processing is critical for any Nigerian e-commerce site. The standard options are:

  • Paystack — The most popular payment gateway in Nigeria. Reliable, well-documented, and trusted by customers.
  • Flutterwave — Another major gateway with good international payment support.
  • Bank transfers — Many Nigerian customers still prefer direct bank transfers. A good e-commerce site should display bank details and confirm transfers automatically.
  • Pay on delivery — For physical products, offering cash-on-delivery can increase conversion rates in certain Nigerian markets.

Your developer should integrate payment options that match how your customers actually want to pay.

Additional E-commerce Costs to Consider

Beyond development, budget for:

  • Payment gateway fees — Paystack charges around 1.5% + ₦100 per transaction. Factor this into your pricing.
  • SSL certificate — Essential for e-commerce. Some hosting includes this, otherwise budget ₦15,000 – ₦30,000 yearly.
  • Product photography — Professional product images significantly increase sales. Budget for quality photography.
  • Hosting — E-commerce sites need reliable hosting. Budget ₦20,000 – ₦50,000 monthly for good performance.
  • Maintenance — Security updates, payment gateway updates, and feature additions require ongoing attention.

Mobile-First E-commerce Design

In Nigeria, over 70% of online shopping happens on mobile phones. Your e-commerce website must be optimized for mobile from day one. This means:

  • Large, tappable buttons and easy navigation
  • Fast loading even on slower connections
  • Simplified checkout process (fewer steps, fewer form fields)
  • WhatsApp integration for customer support during checkout
  • Mobile-optimized product images and descriptions

Security Considerations for Nigerian E-commerce

Online security builds trust with Nigerian customers. Your e-commerce site needs:

  • SSL encryption — All data transmitted between your site and customers must be encrypted.
  • Secure payment processing — Never store card details yourself. Use trusted gateways like Paystack.
  • Data protection — Comply with Nigerian data protection regulations for customer information.
  • Regular security updates — Keep your platform, plugins, and dependencies updated.

Building vs. Using E-commerce Platforms

You have two main approaches: using existing e-commerce platforms (Shopify, WooCommerce) or building a custom solution. Platforms are faster to launch but limit customization. Custom solutions cost more but give you complete control over features, branding, and scalability.

For Nigerian businesses with unique requirements — custom payment flows, specific shipping logic, or integration with existing systems — a custom build often makes more sense long-term.

Getting Started with Your E-commerce Project

Before requesting a quote, prepare:

  • Your product catalog (how many products, categories, variants)
  • Must-have features (wishlist, reviews, discount codes, etc.)
  • Payment methods you want to accept
  • Shipping and delivery strategy
  • Integration needs (accounting software, inventory systems, etc.)

The more specific you can be about your requirements, the more accurate your quote will be.
