How to Choose the Best Website Developer in Nigeria for Your Business

Omoruyi Isaiah — Lambo Code Benin City, Nigeria
Published August 2026

Choosing a website developer is one of the most important business decisions you'll make. A good developer becomes a long-term partner who helps your business grow online. A bad developer wastes your money, delays your launch, and creates problems that cost more to fix later.

This guide covers exactly what to look for when choosing a website developer in Nigeria, questions to ask, and how to evaluate whether someone is the right fit for your business.

Start With Your Business Needs

Before you even look for developers, be clear about what you need. This helps you communicate effectively and evaluate whether a developer can deliver. Ask yourself:

  • What type of website do I need? — Business site, e-commerce, booking system, restaurant ordering, or something else?
  • What functionality is essential? — Payment integration, booking forms, customer accounts, blog, or specific features?
  • What's my budget range? — Knowing this helps you find developers who work within your range.
  • When do I need it launched? — Some developers can deliver quickly, others have longer lead times.
  • Will I need ongoing support? — Some businesses need regular updates; others need a set-and-forget solution.

Having clear answers to these questions makes the entire process smoother.

Evaluate Their Portfolio

A developer's portfolio tells you more than their website ever will. Look for:

  • Live, working websites — Not mockups or designs. Actual sites you can visit and test.
  • Projects similar to yours — If you need a restaurant site, look for restaurant sites they've built.
  • Mobile responsiveness — Open their portfolio sites on your phone. Do they work well?
  • Loading speed — Fast sites indicate good technical practices.
  • Design quality — Do their sites look professional and modern?
  • Functionality — If they claim to build booking systems, test one. Does it actually work?

Be skeptical of developers who can't show you real projects or only show template-based work.

Check Their Technical Skills

You don't need to be technical to evaluate this, but you should understand what they're bringing to the table. Ask about:

  • Frontend vs. full-stack — Can they handle both the visual design and the backend functionality?
  • Technologies they use — React, Node.js, MongoDB, WordPress, or something else? Are these appropriate for your project?
  • Payment integration experience — Have they integrated Paystack or Flutterwave before?
  • Database experience — If you need user accounts or booking systems, do they have database experience?
  • SEO knowledge — Can they build your site to be search-engine friendly?

A good developer can explain these in plain English without overwhelming you with jargon.

Assess Communication and Responsiveness

Communication problems are the #1 reason web projects fail. During your initial interactions, evaluate:

  • Response time — Do they reply within 24 hours? Slower response during sales often means slower response during your project.
  • Clarity of communication — Do they explain things clearly or use confusing technical language?
  • Questions they ask — Good developers ask detailed questions about your business and needs. Poor developers give generic quotes without understanding your project.
  • Availability — Are they available for calls or WhatsApp conversations when needed?

WhatsApp is particularly important in Nigeria — a developer who's responsive on WhatsApp will likely be responsive during your project.

Understand Their Pricing Structure

Pricing transparency is crucial. A good developer should provide:

  • Detailed quotes — Breakdown of what's included, not just a single number.
  • Payment terms — How much upfront, how much on delivery, and any milestone payments.
  • What's not included — Hosting, domain, images, content creation, or ongoing maintenance.
  • Revision policy — How many rounds of changes are included?
  • Timeline — When will each phase be completed?

Be cautious of developers who demand 100% payment upfront or can't commit to a timeline.

Ask About Their Process

Every developer has a process. Understanding theirs helps you know what to expect. Ask about:

  • Discovery phase — Will they take time to understand your business before building?
  • Design process — Will they show you mockups or wireframes before coding?
  • Development approach — Do they build in phases or all at once?
  • Testing process — How do they ensure the site works before launch?
  • Launch process — How do they handle going live?
  • Post-launch support — What happens if something breaks after launch?

A clear, structured process is a sign of an experienced developer.

Check for Nigerian Market Understanding

A developer who understands the Nigerian market will build a better site for you. Look for:

  • Local payment gateway experience — Paystack, Flutterwave, and other Nigerian payment systems.
  • Mobile-first approach — Understanding that most Nigerian users are on mobile.
  • Connectivity awareness — Optimizing for slower internet connections.
  • Local business context — Understanding how Nigerian businesses actually operate.

This local knowledge makes a significant difference in how effective your website will be.

Questions to Ask Every Developer

When you talk to developers, ask these questions:

  • Can you show me 3-5 live websites you've built for businesses similar to mine?
  • What's your typical timeline for a project like this?
  • What's included in your pricing, and what costs extra?
  • How do you handle revisions and changes during development?
  • What happens if I need support after the site launches?
  • How do you handle payment integration (Paystack, Flutterwave)?
  • Will the site be mobile-responsive?
  • Who owns the code and files when the project is complete?
  • Can you provide references from past clients?
  • What's your preferred communication method during projects?

Red Flags to Watch For

Be cautious if a developer:

  • Can't show you any real, live projects
  • Demands full payment before starting work
  • Gives you a quote without asking detailed questions about your needs
  • Communicates very slowly or disappears for days
  • Only uses templates and doesn't build custom solutions
  • Makes unrealistic promises (e.g., "guaranteed #1 on Google")
  • Doesn't have a professional website themselves
  • Refuses to put anything in writing

Trust Your Instincts

Beyond all the practical considerations, pay attention to how you feel about working with this person. You'll likely be working with them for weeks or months. Do they seem trustworthy? Do they listen to you? Do you feel comfortable asking questions?

The best developer-client relationships are partnerships. If something feels off during the sales process, it won't get better during the project.
